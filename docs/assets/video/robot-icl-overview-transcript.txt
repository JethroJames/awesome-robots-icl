Male: A robot watches a demonstration. Has it learned the task, or just copied the motion?

Female: That's the question behind our survey, In-Context Learning for Robots: Methods and Applications. We map how demonstrations, corrections, and experience shape behavior without updating the model's weights during deployment.

Male: So where does that context go?

Female: Into four kinds of execution: predicting actions, transferring geometric motion, planning with world models, or composing skills and programs.

Male: Why does that distinction matter?

Female: Because the same video can guide different mechanisms. Each one has different requirements for transfer, and different ways to fail.

Male: If success goes up, isn't that enough?

Female: Try two tests. Keep the scene fixed and change the demonstration. Then keep the task fixed and change the object. Does behavior follow the teaching, and does it transfer?

Male: For example, picking up a mug by its handle?

Female: Exactly. Reaching the goal is only part of the story. The robot should also preserve the demonstrated contact and action order.

Male: And what should researchers take away?

Female: A framework for comparing methods, designing stronger evaluations, and asking whether experience helps with the next task. Explore the full survey and our continuously updated literature collection.
